# B2 Fair Tickets

**Locked deployment.** Use **Foundry Anvil 1.7.1**, a standalone local EVM chain, with Solidity 0.8.30, OpenZeppelin Contracts 5.4.0 and Node 22 / ethers 6.15.0. Anvil is the chain for development, the volume run, and judging. Start from pre-funded local accounts. No fork RPC, public testnet, faucet, private RPC subscription, bridge, or real gas funds enter the critical path. Use chain ID 31337, Shanghai, a 30 million block gas limit, and loopback RPC. Local gas units and dev ETH still exist and every write must be mined. [Anvil documentation](https://www.getfoundry.sh/anvil/index.html)

## Why the blockchain is essential

Deploy one immutable `FairTickets` contract per event. It owns the entry list, draw result, ticket inventory, current ownership, resale rules and native-asset settlement, and the one-time admission bit. The wallet-to-contract flow is the product. No service can mint, settle a resale, transfer, or authorise entry by writing a database. Every primary sale and resale changes the contract-owned ticket and records payment proceeds in the **same EVM transaction**. The gate signs and sends its own transaction and opens on a verified, successful admission receipt. The independent venue can use the contract and confirm ownership and admission without the organiser's web service or service credentials. If the chain is unavailable, purchases, ownership changes, and new admissions stop.

For the demo, put organiser, identity attester, fan A, fan B and gate keys in separate clients. Demonstrate switching off the optional UI/API and executing the lifecycle with the direct contract client. Publish the chain ID, event contract address, ABI and code hash for judge verification. The local Anvil operator still controls the node and block production. Independent signing and contract enforcement are in scope. Production decentralisation, public finality, audit-proof randomness and real fiat settlement require separate validation.

## Contract and lifecycle

**Scope.** One active general-admission event, 100 slots, at most one held ticket per verified subject, mock identity attestation, native dev ETH settlement, and no platform fees. The face price is in integer paise for display. In the demo only, the contract uses 1 dev wei per displayed paise. Sample face price ₹2,000 means 200,000 dev wei of value plus separate dev gas. No ERC-20, NFT marketplace integration, external oracle, token approval flow, generic transfer route, upgrade proxy, or SQL transaction authority. The ticket is an event-scoped contract record, with ownership transfer only inside the resale function.

| Operation | Contract rule |
|---|---|
| `enter(subject, attestation)` | Identity attester signs the event-scoped opaque subject, wallet address, and expiry using EIP-712. The wallet submits its own transaction. Verify signer, expiry, sender, and entry deadline. Enforce uniqueness on both subject and wallet, bind them once, then append that wallet to the entrant array. Duplicate requests cannot increase entries or draw weight. Attestations use demo data with no PII and no stable cross-event identifier. |
| `lockDraw()`, `draw()` | After entry closes, anyone can lock one target block at current block + 2. Draw once, with `target < block.number <= target + 256` and a nonzero target hash. Mix that hash, contract address and frozen entry count. If the window is missed, fail closed. Entry never charged anyone. Use partial Fisher–Yates against the entrant array for the first min(capacity, entrants) slots and publish the target hash. Persist the selected wallets and fixed claim and resale deadlines in the draw transaction. At 100 slots the draw work is bounded by 100 iterations even with 100,000 entrants. On local Anvil the operator can influence randomness. |
| `claim()` payable | Only a selected wallet can claim its reserved slot, before the claim deadline, at exact face price. Mark the immutable original claim per wallet, set slot owner and version 1, and credit the organiser's proceeds atomically. Repeated or losing transactions revert before creating a second sale or taking payment value. Unclaimed slots stay unissued and are counted at the deadline. |
| `list(slot, expectedVersion, price)` | During resale only, verify current holder, version and unredeemed state. Price must be within zero and the lower of face price and the current owner's paid price. Store one immutable listing per slot/version. No direct-recipient transfer. Unfilled listings expire at cutoff and the seller keeps the ticket. |
| `buy(slot, listedVersion, expectedPrice)` payable | During resale only, require a different registered buyer with no held ticket. Match live listing, seller, version, expected price and exact value. Recheck both caps. Close the listing, credit seller proceeds, increment ticket version, set the new owner and paid price to the exact resale value, and update ownership indexes in one transaction. Keep purchase outcomes indexed by slot and listed version in `Resold` events so further transfers cannot erase them. A subsequent holder can relist. Resale is first-come. |
| `redeem(slot, version, expiry, ownerSig, scanId)` | Only the event's gate wallet can redeem during the gate window. Require an issued ticket, exact current version, unused admission, and `block.timestamp <= expiry <= block.timestamp + 120 seconds`. Verify an EIP-712 signature from the current holder over slot, version and expiry under the chain-and-contract domain. Mark the ticket admitted and emit `Admitted(slot, version, scanId)`. Replays and losing scans revert. Ambiguous confirmation holds entry for staff. |
| `withdraw()` | Each payee withdraws only their accrued proceeds. Set the credit to zero before the external value call, and revert the whole withdrawal on failure. Ticket purchases never call out to a recipient, so a seller cannot block valid settlement by refusing a payout. |

Entry closes at a preset chain timestamp. Draw starts once entry is closed. CLAIM runs from draw until claim deadline, RESALE runs from that deadline up to resale cutoff, and GATE runs from cutoff to gate close. Store all deadlines once and use `block.timestamp` on every state transition. Terminate an expired draw fixture explicitly. Use test-only mining controls for boundary tests and remove those controls from normal clients. Gate signatures prove possession of a fresh credential. A copy stolen inside its short validity window can still be presented first. Use OpenZeppelin EIP712/ECDSA and include an immutable random edition ID in both typed payloads, so another fixture with the same chain ID, deployment address and keys still has a separate signature namespace. Pin the edition in the clients. [EIP-712](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-712) · [OpenZeppelin cryptography](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/contracts/5.x/api/utils#cryptography)

**Submission and retries.** Independent wallets sign their own writes. Serialize nonce allocation per signer. Journal the operation ID, nonce, signed bytes and derived hash **before first broadcast**, in private local storage. For identical retries, recover the hash and receipt or reconcile against the original claim and versioned sale events. Only rebroadcast identical signed bytes while an outcome is unresolved. Never generate a new payable transaction to recover a lost response. Distinguish pending, committed, rejected and unavailable. Reverts never count toward successful throughput.

**Gate action.** Generate a unique scan ID per station operation and use that ID when resuming its journal. Confirm status 1 for the exact locally signed hash and a matching `Admitted(slot, version, scanId)` log from the pinned contract before publishing ADMIT. Atomically mark that scan ID as acted on in local station storage before showing green. Only the worker that records this first action can show green. If persistence succeeds but the green signal is lost, or if a receipt is unavailable, hold the guest for staff. Verify this path by interrupting the client before receipt and before showing green.

**Local isolation.** Launch a dedicated chain for the demo. Use independent generated keys for all app roles, not the node's unlocked stock signer. Fund them in fixture setup. Keep keys and the RPC port local. If multiple devices need access, put a narrow proxy in front that allows reads and raw signed submission and blocks unlocked-account sends, account impersonation, state changes and mining controls. Never store keys or live admission credentials in source control or published logs. Developers can use Anvil controls on a disposable load chain only. Prefer a fresh named fixture per benchmark. Keep the judging chain running and preserve its contract/event evidence. Anvil state export may aid recovery, but a reload must be treated as a fresh run unless contract state, block/time and required receipts are confirmed. | Original benchmark | What passes on chain |
|---|---|
| **Critical. Fair allocation** | Two wallets with attestations for the same subject produce one entrant. With 1,000 unique eligible entrants, verify 100 distinct winners, one reserved slot per winner, no late entry, no losing claim, and no draw rerun. Verify draw results independently from the frozen input and recorded target hash. |
| **Critical. Same-subject duplicate-entry resistance** | Send 1,000 repeated entry requests for one verified subject. Every resolved request refers to the original entry or is rejected. Verify the on-chain count and draw weight remain one. Include separately signed attempts exercising the contract directly, so client deduplication cannot hide a contract defect. |
| **Critical. Overselling** | Dispatch 1,000 checkout requests in one client burst against 100 slots. Use 1,000 registered wallets and one signed claim each so the verdict reaches the contract. Exactly 100 sales and 900 losing claims, with 100 unique owners, 100 committed sale receipts, and payment proceeds for exactly 100. In a second fixture burst ten attempts from each winner to test replay safety. Record client peak, transaction outcomes and final state. |
| **Critical. Capped resale** | Reject above-face and mismatched buyer price with no ownership or proceeds change. Execute a discounted sale and a lower-price onward resale, then reject a relist above the most recent paid price and below face price. Test races of two buyers for one listing and one buyer for two tickets. A failed payout attempt cannot change ticket ownership. |
| **High. Transfer invalidation** | Sign as A for a gate-valid expiry, transfer A's ticket to B, then reject A's stale credential at the gate and verify B's fresh credential. In another fixture transfer A → B → A and verify A's original version stays invalid. Inspect owner, version and proceeds against contract data. |
| **High. Forgery** | Reject unknown slots, a changed signature or payload, a valid signature for a different contract/chain or edition, and redemption from a non-gate wallet. Verify zero admission events. |
| **Critical. One-time admission** | Dispatch 100 concurrent scan attempts for the same credential and distinct scan IDs. Verify 1 successful admission receipt and 99 already-used denials, plus one stored redemption. Assign consecutive nonces to direct staff submissions, mine nonce prefixes in order, and capture receipt status. Count gas spent by failed attempts separately. |

Run a single scenario continuously from entry and draw through primary payment, price-cap rejection, legitimate resale, seller rejection and buyer admission. Add deadline boundary, expired signature, unknown/lost transaction outcome and chain-disconnect checks. Test that native value is credited correctly and withdrawal matches the credit. Track client errors separately from expected contract reverts and reconcile successful events against state. Expected failures may be validated by receipt plus trace or a direct call establishing the reason under the same state. No simulated rejection substitutes for a direct transaction in the 1,000 genuine distinct-wallet checkout case.

## Volume decision and verification

The screenshot's 100,000-in-20-hours target is **1.39 successful writes/s** across the event. Use the stricter interpretation until the judging metric is clarified: **100,000 distinct successful, mined, state-changing contract transactions**, each with a distinct hash and a matching status-1 receipt/event. No reads, deploys, prefunding, failed attempts, receipt polls or duplicate hashes count. A JSON-RPC batch is a transport envelope for distinct signed transactions. A single contract batch is still one transaction.

**Verified Windows install path.** The generic Anvil npm wrapper's postinstall failed under Windows cmd. Install the pinned official platform binary package directly, along with the compiler and ethers. The 100,000-write probe launches its own local process, funds four native signers at genesis, signs real EVM transactions, supplies 100 separate transactions per block, verifies each receipt and checks the final contract counter. The probe contract writes three storage slots per operation with an additional counter and event. It is a capacity measurement, with actual `FairTickets` gas and performance to be measured separately.

**Measured on this Windows machine.** The first complete capacity run mined **100,000 distinct successful contract writes in 370.39 seconds (269.99/s)**. The final on-chain counter was 100,000 and the largest observed transaction used 111,896 gas. The timer includes signing, submission, mining and receipt verification. A separate burst check funded 1,000 independent wallets with batched `anvil_setBalance`, sent 1,000 native transfers together and reconciled all 1,000 receipts and the recipient balance. `FairTickets` and its ticket-specific acceptance suite remain implementation milestones.

The evidence-export run repeated all 100,000 writes in **399.40 seconds (250.37/s)**. A second process verified the receipt archive's SHA-256, 100,000 unique successful receipt records, consecutive issued slots and 1,000 mined blocks. The judging launch was separately checked for chain ID, block gas limit and one-second interval mining.

## Product and validation

BookMyShow already randomises initial waiting-room positions and offers original-price-based resale for selected events with a replacement booking ID. Ticketmaster supports ticket requests, face-value exchange and rotating barcodes on eligible events. FIFA has operated a fixed-window draw plus an official resale marketplace. B2's specific pitch for a small independent organiser is an asynchronous, opt-in claim window paired with wallet-held tickets, contract-settled resale and a separately operated gate that verifies admission on the same chain. [BookMyShow queue](https://support.bookmyshow.com/support/solutions/articles/4000232931-why-am-i-in-the-queue-) · [BookMyShow terms](https://in.bookmyshow.com/static/terms-and-conditions/) · [Ticketmaster Face Value Exchange](https://help.ticketmaster.com/hc/en-us/articles/9781464415249-How-does-Ticketmaster-s-Face-Value-Exchange-work) · [FIFA draw and resale](https://tickets.fifa.com/organisation/media-releases/over-500-million-ticket-requests-world-cup-2026-random-selection-draw)

| User | What to measure |
|---|---|
| Fans registering or returning to claim | Five people per mobile task. Target four of five completing unassisted in ≤90 active seconds per task. In 200 synthetic draws of 500 early and 500 late entrants for 100 seats, expect roughly 10% in each group and target an aggregate gap ≤2 percentage points. This checks draw outcome conditional on eligibility, so separately count registration and verification failures. |
| Resale sellers and buyers | Count above-cap requests rejected. In a later pilot measure eligible listings sold by cutoff and median time to sale. Proposed targets are ≥70% and ≤24 hours for multi-day listings. Record wallet friction and buyer total outlay. |
| Organisers | Ask one independent organiser whether they would run a dry run. Measure claimed/reserved at deadline and unissued/capacity. At 80 out of 100 claimed and ₹2,000 face price, 20 unissued slots represent ₹40,000 of face-value inventory unsold. |
| Gate staff | Two staff, 20 scripted decisions. Target ≥18/20 correct unaided, median decision time under 3 seconds, and zero incorrect admits. Report failed or pending inclusions and staff referrals. |

This prototype proves one entry per attested subject, not one human per identity in production. Group bookings, real identity verification, off-platform side payments, account or wallet sharing, real payouts, accessibility and venue network operations remain pilot work. A single local node can reset, stall, or be controlled by its operator. Demonstrate on a private judging machine with loopback RPC and a recorded backup run.
